**What happened** *(AI-drafted)*

Signed in as alice, each of leaderboard_weekly_xp, leaderboard_daily_challenge, leaderboard_strategy_accuracy and leaderboard_tournament_wins returned a row for erin — a seeded account whose profile privacy is 'private' — carrying her display name next to her score or wins. The approved expectation of each row was a projection with no private-privacy account's identity. All four functions are SECURITY DEFINER and join public.profiles with no privacy or block condition (Arc21 source, 20260905120000_bounded_client_reads.sql), so the policy that withholds erin's profile row from alice, profiles_select_visible, is never consulted.

**Why it matters** *(AI-drafted, grounded in the approved matrix and the frozen ratings)*

The approved rows name the escalation: "returning a private-privacy account's identity". The frozen WP2 rating makes public.profiles High-tier because its privacy column decides who may read the profile (RUBRIC Part 4.2; cp3-tiers.json). A player who chose 'private' is still identifiable, by display name, to every signed-in player on four boards. The exposure needs no special knowledge: any account can call the functions (anonymous callers are refused, F5.272).

> Approved matrix, "Escalation would be": returning a private-privacy account's identity, or being callable without an account at all. WP0's own pgTAP gate treats granting this to anon as a breach
> Approved matrix, "Escalation would be": returning a private account's identity, or leaking seed_secret through the join

**Remediation options** *(AI-drafted and presented at CP4 — not implemented. The author accepted this item as design intent, so these are reference options, not open work)*

1. Exclude private-privacy accounts from every leaderboard's result except the caller's own row — a privacy condition in each function (for example `p.privacy <> 'private' or t.user_id = auth.uid()`).
   *Tradeoffs:* Private players vanish from global standings. Applied before ranking, other players' ranks renumber around them; applied after, a gap in the rank sequence reveals that a hidden entry exists. Four functions must change consistently, and `my_leaderboard_position` must agree with them on who is ranked.
2. Keep private accounts ranked but anonymise their row for everyone except that account: display name replaced (for example 'Private player'), avatar withheld; optionally shown in full to friends for 'friends' privacy.
   *Tradeoffs:* Standings stay complete and consistent, but the score and rank of a private player are still disclosed, unattributed. Needs a single shared helper so the four functions cannot drift apart.
3. Accept as design intent and record it: 'private' governs the profile row and statistics, not competitive standings — as the settings note already tells players.
   *Tradeoffs:* No code change. The four WP3 rows keep reporting `deficient` against their frozen expectation until that expectation is re-approved in a new planning run — a planning change, not a result change.
